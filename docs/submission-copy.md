# Colosseum submission copy

**Product name:** ExitPlan

**One-liner:** Read-only pre-flight for unwinding a SOL hedge: checks Hyperliquid's own margin rule on HyperCore, simulates the Solana legs and builds the unsigned order before you sign.

**Description (≤ 1,500 characters):**
Hedged SOL on Solana, short on Hyperliquid, need cash? It reads your Hyperliquid account from HyperCore's info API, recomputes withdrawable with the exchange's transfer rule and builds the unsigned reduce-only IOC order from the live l2Book; on Solana it builds your Kamino withdrawal and Jupiter swap and runs both through simulateTransaction. Nothing is signed or sent. Enter a target and the rules you won't break: debt cap, keep net exposure, keep SOL, minimum margin buffer. ExitPlan reads both chains by address and verifies the Hyperliquid account mode: Standard and Unified accounts are supported; portfolio margin is rejected. It evaluates every route from wallet USDC and liquidity-capped Kamino withdrawals to paired spot-and-short trims sized in the exchange's 0.01 SOL lot, short reductions that release margin, and borrowing as a comparison. Margin follows Hyperliquid's transfer rule, and on the live accounts we tested the model's withdrawable matched the exchange's within a dollar, to the cent on the standard example. Each route shows fees at your own taker rate, the yearly carry it gives up, and the liquidation price before and after. The rehearsal simulates the Jupiter swap and the Kamino withdrawal with simulateTransaction, links each unsigned transaction to Solana Explorer's inspector, and walks the live Hyperliquid order book to build the inner reduce-only order for the buy-back. An AI panel turns plain English into constraints you confirm. Nothing is signed or sent.

**Problem:** Phantom put Hyperliquid perps inside a Solana wallet, so a common SOL position now spans two chains: SOL and USDC (often in Kamino) on Solana, and a Hyperliquid short that hedges it and earns funding. When you need cash, every lever breaks something: sell SOL and you are net short, pull margin and your liquidation price drops, borrow and you carry debt you swore off. The state lives in three apps, and none of the three apps shows what one leg does to the other two.

**Why now (verified 2026-10-11 unless marked):**
- The hedge is big and it pays: Hyperliquid's SOL perp holds about 6.0M SOL (~$669M) of open interest and funding paid shorts about 9.1% annualized over Oct 5–12 (Hyperliquid info API, verified 2026-10-12).
- Unified accounts (Hyperliquid, 2026) let one spot USDC balance back every perp, which makes "how much can I take out?" harder to read, not easier; ExitPlan reads the spot state and matches the exchange's withdrawable on a live unified account (verified 2026-10-12).
- The cash path exists: Circle documents withdrawing USDC from a HyperCore perp balance straight into a Solana token account through CCTP (`sendToEvmWithData`, destination domain 5); ExitPlan lists this leg with a $1 placeholder fee and does not simulate it.
- Exit liquidity is not guaranteed: Kamino's main-market USDC reserve was 93.2% utilized ($112.5M borrowed of $120.7M), leaving about $8.2M withdrawable (Kamino API).

**Wedge:** A read-only pre-flight for the most common hedged structure (SOL spot + wallet/Kamino USDC + Hyperliquid SOL short) on Standard and Unified Hyperliquid accounts, with explanations and real Solana simulations, before any execution product. Hyperliquid shows one account's withdrawable, Kamino one deposit, Jupiter one quote; ExitPlan checks all three legs against one set of rules. The scope is deliberately one structure done end to end: the engine knows nothing about venues (it takes a portfolio shape and returns every route with its reasons), so another Kamino market, another lender or a HIP-3 position is one adapter function, not an engine change.

**Target user:** SOL funding farmers with $5k–$500k delta-neutral positions split between Solana (or Phantom) and Hyperliquid who need cash without accidentally breaking the hedge; later, wallets and trading front ends serving those users. Users so far: [[N]] live imports on their own addresses (Vercel Analytics, counted on submission day).

**Business model:** The pre-flight is free; no paying users yet. Stage one, a watch tier at $10/month: a standing pre-flight on a position that alerts when the plan stops being feasible, carry flips or liquidation gets close (the recurring need is the watch, not the unwind). Stage two, the same check embedded as a drop-in sheet in wallets and front-ends that already earn builder fees, on a revenue share. Stage three, once execution ships, a Hyperliquid builder code of a few bps on the short leg plus a Jupiter platform fee on the Solana leg (a $10k unwind at 5 bps is $5; a thousand unwinds a month is $5k, a floor, not the plan). Thirty-day milestone: twenty watch-tier sign-ups and one wallet or front-end integration conversation.

**Why it fits the Hyperliquid track:** ExitPlan reads a Hyperliquid account through the public info API, confirms its Standard or Unified mode with `userAbstraction` (and establishes a `default` setting from the balances), and on the live accounts we tested its free-collateral figure matched the exchange's own withdrawable: on 2026-10-12, $5,444.93 against $5,444.94 on a Standard account and $15,436.60 to the cent on a Unified account. Every short reduction is sized in the 0.01 SOL lot read from `meta`, priced at the user's own `userFees` taker rate plus a 10 bps execution allowance, and checked against the $10 minimum. The rehearsal then walks the live `l2Book` and shows the inner unsigned reduce-only IOC order (asset index, size, tick-rounded limit) the user would sign, withholds it when the book is stale, and never sends it.

**HyperCore integration (verifiable facts):** ExitPlan calls HyperCore's public info endpoint by address, with no key: `userAbstraction` (Standard and Unified accepted, portfolio margin and vaults rejected with the reason), `clearinghouseState`, `spotClearinghouseState`, `userFees`, `meta`, `fundingHistory` and `l2Book`. Free collateral reserves max(initial margin, 10% of notional) per position, the exchange's transfer rule; on 2026-10-12 it returned $5,444.93 against the exchange's $5,444.94 withdrawable (Standard) and $15,436.60 against $15,436.60 (Unified). The rehearsal emits the inner order `{a, b, p, s, r: true, t: {limit: {tif: "Ioc"}}}`, sized in `meta`'s 0.01 SOL lot, priced at 5 significant figures, capped at the slippage allowance. It is never signed or sent.

**Public good:** ExitPlan is MIT-licensed. `web/engine.mjs` is a dependency-free ES module with no I/O: pass a portfolio and constraints, get every route with its rejection reasons. `bin/exitplan.mjs` prints the same pre-flight report in a terminal. It calls public endpoints only, needs no keys, signs nothing, and runs against any Solana and Hyperliquid address.

**Why it fits the Solana track:** Positions come from Solana RPC, the Kamino API and Jupiter by address; the rehearsal builds the real Kamino withdrawal with the Kamino SDK and the real Jupiter swap, runs both through `simulateTransaction`, reports what would arrive against the plan, and links each unsigned transaction to Solana Explorer's inspector. The route engine and report are dependency-free modules that also run from the terminal.

**Blockchains integrated:** Solana — mainnet RPC (getBalance, simulateTransaction), Kamino Lend main-market API (obligations, reserve liquidity and supply APY) and Kamino SDK (unsigned withdraw transaction build, server side), Jupiter (quote, unsigned swap build, wallet balances), a read-only wallet connect for the Solana and EVM addresses (addresses only; no signing). Hyperliquid — HyperCore info API (clearinghouseState, spotClearinghouseState for unified accounts, userAbstraction account-mode verification, vaultDetails, allMids, fundingHistory, userFees, meta, l2Book, liquidation price). Circle CCTP HyperCore→Solana is modelled, not executed. HyperEVM: not used.

**Tools:** Solana JSON-RPC via publicnode and the public mainnet RPC, Kamino API and @kamino-finance/klend-sdk, Jupiter lite API, Hyperliquid info API, Phantom provider (read-only), OpenAI Responses API (structured output, server side), Vercel Functions, Node test runner (279 tests, `npm test`, no network), LinkeDOM.
